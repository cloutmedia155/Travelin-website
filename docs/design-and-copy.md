# Homepage design and copy rationale

## Objective

Help people arriving from Instagram and paid ads picture the experience, understand who hosts it, compare dates and prices, and choose a trip. Most guests book solo; friends and couples also join general trips. Price and paid time off are the confirmed concerns. Olivia personally hosts every departure and helps people connect.

The visual reference is White Desert’s sustained imagery, restrained editorial text, generous space and transitions between scenes. The door scene is the specifically requested Travel Next Level reference. Destination cards follow the overlapping composition in the Travel Sensations screenshot. This is a new implementation, not a copy of those sites’ code.

## Section sequence

| Section | Visitor question | Copy and visual decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero | Is this for me? | Start with “Go somewhere new with people who are up for it.” The door moves with scrolling. Left-aligned explanatory copy appears next, then the trip button as the plane appears. No audience-label eyebrow. |
| Experience intro | What sits behind the social videos? | Smaller serif heading and specific everyday moments: days out, dinner, dancing and time alone. The off-center composition slows the pace without requiring dense copy. |
| Stay | What am I paying for? | Explain the 4–5-star stay choice. Three tilted images sit below the words so photography does not block reading. Stock villa photographs illustrate atmosphere, not a promised booked hotel. |
| Days out | How much do I have to organize? | State what is arranged and direct readers to the actual itinerary. The next photos arrive from the left in the same scene. |
| People | Will arriving alone feel awkward? | Describe Olivia’s introductions and room for different personalities. Use real client group photos instead of generic models. |
| Experience film | Can I see what the group feels like? | Comes after all three chapters. The frame grows toward full screen, with cloud edges. Playback with sound is a deliberate action. |
| Trips | Where, when, and how much? | Four destination tabs and a larger central card. Dates, duration and price stay outside the image for legibility. No invented urgency or availability. |
| Traveler stories | Who actually joins? | Real group images with space between cards. The three positions await supplied video testimonials; no fabricated quotes or play buttons. |
| Founder | Who will be there with me? | Olivia’s actual portrait, her role as planner and host, and her ability to get people talking. Working story awaits her own written account. |
| Photo collage | What might I remember? | Real trip moments reveal around a central photograph as the visitor scrolls. Caption remains a draft that can be changed separately from the animation. |
| Questions | What could stop me booking? | Start with price and time off, followed by solo/friend travel, rooms, optional activities and cancellation. Accordion avoids presenting all detail at once. |
| Booking | What happens after I choose? | Three practical steps joined by a curved growing botanical path: review, reserve, prepare. The path supports the sequence without hiding the instructions. |
| Final CTA | What should I do now? | Retain the client’s strong Instagram line. “Choose Your Trip” returns to the trip selector, with a quieter question link alongside. |

## Language rules

Use concrete actions and observable details. Prefer “Olivia comes on every trip” to vague claims about exceptional service. Avoid unsupported superlatives, invented testimonial quotations, false scarcity and guaranteed friendships. Explain inclusions on the relevant trip page instead of implying every package includes the same activities or meals.

Cormorant Garamond supplies the light serif and italic contrast; Manrope carries practical reading. The new hero is approximately 58px at a 1348px viewport, instead of the first version’s much larger display text. Smaller labels and restrained body widths keep the image dominant.

These choices are design hypotheses, not proven conversion results. After launch, evaluate trip clicks, trip-page engagement and booking completion by device and traffic source before claiming a conversion uplift.
